# The Verification Crisis and the Role of AI

The intersection of mathematics and artificial intelligence is currently navigating a period of profound uncertainty[cite: 1]. As large language models become increasingly capable of generating solutions to complex problems, the bottleneck in both mathematics and software development has shifted dramatically toward human verification[cite: 1]. 

AI companies have recently demonstrated the ability to formalize fields-level mathematics—such as Viazovska's sphere-packing theorem—by feeding the prior work of human mathematicians into proprietary autonomous agents[cite: 1]. This milestone, however, sparked significant controversy within the formalization community[cite: 1]. Prominent open-source developers warned that such aggressive, corporate-driven automation could turn the ecosystem into a "giant radioactive wasteland," effectively destroying the collaborative and educational benefits of formalization projects[cite: 1]. 

This tension exposes a deeper cognitive risk[cite: 1]. The training required to do mathematical research does not merely come from understanding explanations; it is forged by the difficult struggle to produce them[cite: 1]. When an AI chatbot can instantly resolve a complex exercise in Homotopy Type Theory, explaining every detail perfectly, it creates the ultimate recipe for not learning[cite: 1]. 

If students and researchers continually delegate the intellectual burden of solving problems from scratch, we risk a future where there are no human experts left with the deep, structural intuition required to extend the theory[cite: 1]. While AI is highly proficient at applying rules and generating code, it does not currently know how to pose fundamentally new problems or push a theoretical framework into uncharted territory[cite: 1]. If we rely entirely on automated assistants for verification, the very foundations of scientific certainty could stagnate, leaving us dependent on tools we no longer fully understand[cite: 1].
