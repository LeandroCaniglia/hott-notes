title: The Verification Crisis and the Role of AI
---

The intersection of mathematics and artificial intelligence is currently navigating a period of profound uncertainty. As large language models become increasingly capable of generating solutions to complex problems, the bottleneck in both mathematics and software development has shifted dramatically toward human verification. 

AI companies have recently demonstrated the ability to formalize fields-level mathematics—such as Viazovska's sphere-packing theorem—by feeding the prior work of human mathematicians into proprietary autonomous agents. This milestone, however, sparked significant controversy within the formalization community. Prominent open-source developers warned that such aggressive, corporate-driven automation could turn the ecosystem into a "giant radioactive wasteland," effectively destroying the collaborative and educational benefits of formalization projects. 

This tension exposes a deeper cognitive risk. The training required to do mathematical research does not merely come from understanding explanations; it is forged by the difficult struggle to produce them. When an AI chatbot can instantly resolve a complex exercise in Homotopy Type Theory, explaining every detail perfectly, it creates the ultimate recipe for not learning. 

If students and researchers continually delegate the intellectual burden of solving problems from scratch, we risk a future where there are no human experts left with the deep, structural intuition required to extend the theory. While AI is highly proficient at applying rules and generating code, it does not currently know how to pose fundamentally new problems or push a theoretical framework into uncharted territory. If we rely entirely on automated assistants for verification, the very foundations of scientific certainty could stagnate, leaving us dependent on tools we no longer fully understand.
